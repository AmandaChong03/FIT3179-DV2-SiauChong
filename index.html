Pets and vets in Australia

Who Cares for Australia's 31.6 Million Pets?

Australia has more pets than people.
But who cares for them all?
We follow pets from home to the vet.
We meet the vets and see what care costs.
Then we ask what owners can do.
All costs are in Australian dollars.

1 A nation of pet lovers

Pets are part of family life in Australia.

31.6 million pets

27.9 million people

Pets outnumber people in Australia.
Owners spend generously on their care.
But how much of it reaches the vet?

Pets: Animal Medicines Australia, 2025.
People: Australian Bureau of Statistics, March 2026.

Pets live in 73 of every 100 Australian homes.Each square is one home in a hundred.

Owners spend $21.3 billion a year on pets.Food takes almost half of it.Vet care gets just 9%, about $1.9 billion.

2 Who looks after them

Australia has six states and two territories.
Its vets are not spread evenly across them.

Darker states have more vet businesses per person.
The Northern Territory has the most.
Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory have the fewest.
One business can run several clinics.

Vets train at only seven Australian universities.
Each dot marks one vet school.
Tasmania and both territories have none.

Most young vets are women.

252 women aged 30 to 34, the largest group

From age 65, men outnumber women.

3 A profession stretched thin

Now the problem: Australia is short of vets.
And who owns the clinics is changing.

More vets now work for big company chains.
Their share grew from 2% to 23%.
Most of that growth came in two years.

Small clinics struggle most to hire a vet.
At one-vet clinics, 70% of jobs stayed empty
for over a year.
Only 20 jobs were advertised, so read this carefully.

Across Australia, many vet jobs stay empty for a year.
Bigger circles mean more jobs still empty.
Tasmania (70%) and the Northern Territory fare worst.
Few clinics answered there, so read with care.

So where do sick pets go?
And what does their care cost?

4 Where pets get care, and what for

Most sick pets go to a local clinic.
Their bills are rising, and some are huge.

Three in four vets work at local clinics: 76.3%.
Far fewer work in emergency or specialist hospitals.

The most common problems are everyday ones.
Skin problems top the list for dogs, about $700.
These costs come from insured pets only.
Use the switch to see cats.

An emergency is a different story.
One slipped-disc bill for a dog reached $72,732.
That is about 15 times the average bill.
Insurance only pays up to its yearly limit.

Even everyday care costs more each year.
Treating a stomach upset has doubled in price.
It rose from $457 in 2015 to $956 in 2025.

5 Caring for pets when money is tight

Rising bills put pressure on owners.
When money is tight, some cut back on care.

Some owners save money by cutting vet care.
18% of cat owners and 14% of dog owners did.
Each icon is two owners in every hundred.

Yet prevention costs far less than an emergency.
A year of tick, flea and worm care: $122.
One tick-paralysis bill averages $3,484.

What is tick paralysis?

Paralysis ticks live along Australia's east coast.
Their bite can stop a pet walking or breathing.
Regular treatment helps protect pets.
These costs come from two different reports.

There is good news, too.
Fewer dogs and cats now enter RSPCA shelters.
Dogs fell from 70,514 a year to 15,852.
Rabbits, birds and other animals rose.
Use the menu to see each group alone.

What a responsible owner does

Simple habits keep pets healthy and costs down.

  • Vaccinate
  • Desex
  • Microchip
  • Get a yearly check-up
  • Use tick, flea and worm care

6 What it all means

Three lessons emerge from the data.
They can also help pet owners in Malaysia.

Pets are family

Most Australian homes have a pet.
Owners spend billions on them each year.

Vets are stretched thin

Many vet jobs stay empty for over a year.
Small clinics struggle most to hire.

Prevention pays

Vet bills rise every year.
Prevention costs less than one emergency.
